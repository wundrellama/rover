## Why M7 opens now

Rover reached parity on fuel and exceeded it. The real aCar corpus holds 420 fill
records, and Rover records every one. The same corpus holds 39 service and note
events, 65 service subtype definitions, and 8 reminders. Rover records none of them.

M7 closes that gap and adds vehicle ownership, which neither aCar nor Fuelly
provides.

## Hard gates

**The post-publish rule is in force.** Rover is published and installed on another
ship. New capability enters through a new child or link relation. It never enters
through a new column on a populated relation.

Other standing rules, unchanged:

- Optional data uses an absent child row. It never uses a bunt, a zero, or an empty
  string.
- `archived @f` is written literal `N` on insert. The `@f` bunt is `%.y`.
- IDs are nonzero random 128-bit `@ux`, generated by Rover. Writes use `INSERT`,
  never `UPSERT`.
- All foreign keys are `RESTRICT`.
- Application reads use urQL pokes. Scries are for admin and diagnostics only.
- Arithmetic is exact integer arithmetic. No floating point.
- Raw machine IDs never appear at a human, agent, or remote boundary.

**The Gate 7 fence stays shut.** No task adds a fixture seed, a readback report, or
a diagnostic to the shipping `$action` union. New scaffolding is a probe under
`probes/` or a battery step through Eyre, written that way from the start.

## The common event header

Every vehicle-event family parent carries these columns and no others:

```text
vehicle-id, observed-start, observed-end, observed-precision,
source-zone, recorded-at, plus at most one type-specific reference
```

Every association attaches to the family parent. No association attaches to a typed
child. This covers the odometer link, the station link, cost, tags, payment method,
and notes.

A family without an odometer link is incomplete by definition.

Three parents exist under this rule. `energy-acquisitions` and
`consumable-acquisitions` shipped in the M0 pour. `vehicle-events` arrives in T1.

## Tasks

### T1 — Event family backbone

Create `vehicle-events` and the typed children `service-events`, `expense-events`,
and `note-events`. The children carry identity only. Which child exists is the kind.

Create the parent-keyed associations: cost, station, odometer, tags, payment method,
and notes. Reuse the shipped `tag-definitions`, `payment-method-definitions`,
`places`, and `stations`. Only the link rows are new.

A service or expense total is **entered**, not derived. A shop invoice has no
quantity and no unit price, so there are no operands to multiply. This follows the
shipped `charging-cost-source-totals` precedent for a receipt-total-only charge. It
does not weaken the rule that a fill's total paid is always calculated.

Done-check: a service event with an entered total and a linked odometer reads back
through Eyre, and the reading appears in the vehicle's one `odometer-observations`
list.

### T2 — Service subtype catalog

Create the subtype definition family and the many-to-many link to `vehicle-events`.
Seed a starter pack.

Subtype selection is many-to-many from the start. The real corpus holds one service
record with 10 subtypes and another with 7.

Done-check: one event carries 10 subtypes and renders all 10.

### T3 — Charging odometer repair

Create `energy-acquisition-odometers`, keyed to `energy-acquisitions`. Copy the
existing `fuel-fill-odometers` rows into it. Drop the old table.

This is Rover's first populated-data migration, and it runs on a published app.

1. Take a full pier backup before the migration runs.
2. Record the source row count before the copy.
3. Compare row count and row content after the copy.
4. Drop the old table only after the comparison passes.

Done-check: mileage recorded on a charging session, read back through Eyre. Fill
mileage still reads back for every migrated row.

### T4 — Vehicle acquisition and disposal

Create `vehicle-acquisitions` and `vehicle-disposals` as typed children of
`vehicle-events`. Create the disposal-kind definition family: sold, traded in,
totaled, scrapped, gifted, stolen.

Both children carry an entered total, for the same reason a service total is
entered.

A trade-in writes two independent events. It writes a `%traded-in` disposal on the
old vehicle with its own amount and odometer, and an ordinary purchase on the new
vehicle with its own amount. **No link relation joins them.** The out-of-pocket
figure is a rendering, derived at the boundary from two stored facts.

`archived` keeps its current meaning. It is a display state. A disposal is an event
with a date and an amount.

Done-check: a purchase and a sale on one vehicle, with cost of ownership derived
over the interval between them.

### T5 — Ownership interval derivation

A purchase opens an ownership interval. A disposal closes one. Every interval
derivation runs inside one ownership interval and never crosses a gap between two.
This covers fuel economy, charging efficiency, consumable economy, cost per mile,
and cost of ownership.

This adds Rover's first **derived** break reason. The three shipped reasons are
`%missed-fill`, `%excluded`, and `%owner-marked`, and all three are owner-supplied.
A disposal is already in the database, so Rover honors it without being told.

**The read side must consult the break.** A break row that the statistics screen
ignores renders the impossible number anyway and labels it eligible.

Done-check, and this one decides the task: build a buy-sell-rebuy history where the
cross-gap interval **would be the best economy on record**, then prove it is not
reported as best. A fixture that only proves the break row exists proves nothing.

### T6 — Reminders

Create the reminder relations. A reminder carries a time interval and a distance
interval, each with a due point, and keys to a service subtype.

Distance-due evaluates against the derived current odometer.

This is Rover's first time-driven surface. The agent has no Behn wire today.

Done-check: a reminder falls due by distance after an odometer observation crosses
its threshold, and falls due by time on its date.

### T7 — Vehicle specification

Create typed child relations per concern, following shape (2) in
`app-structure.md`: identifiers, make and model, drivetrain, appearance. Every
column a row defines must be mandatory when that row exists.

**VIN and license plate are identifying personal data.** They never appear in a
fixture, a demo dataset, or a commit. When sharing lands they default to owner-only.
State that default again in the sharing ticket.

Done-check: a vehicle with no VIN evidence gains a verified VIN later, and no
existing link is re-keyed.

### T8 — Definition lifecycle

Add rename and archive endpoints for energy sources, driving modes, consumables,
service subtypes, and disposal kinds. Copy the shipped `archive-custom-field`
pattern.

M7 creates two new definition families, so a create-only M7 would widen this gap
from three families to five.

Done-check: a mistyped definition is archived and disappears from every selector,
while the historical records that reference it still render.

### T9 — Import widening

Extend `tools/acar-import/convert.py` and `desk/lib/rover-import.hoon` to cover
service events, service subtypes, reminders, and vehicle specification.

The real corpus holds 39 events, 65 subtype definitions of which 29 are referenced,
and 8 reminders.

Two corpus findings to handle explicitly:

- The source has duplicate subtype names. `Car Wash` and `Insurance` each appear
  twice, once as a service subtype and once as an expense subtype.
- One source record typed `note` carries a cost of 811.88. The other three notes
  cost zero. The importer reports this record. It does not silently reclassify it.

Done-check: the full corpus imports, and the report names every unmapped record with
a count and a reason.

### T10 — Export

The export format is the import format. Add the read path and the download control.

Done-check: an export re-imports into an empty database and produces the same row
counts.

### T11 — Statistics across every family

Added after the original ten. T9 made every family importable and T4 added ownership,
which together made a whole-cost view possible for the first time.

Statistics reads the selected vehicle's event-family costs and renders total cost of
ownership, cost per distance all-in, spend by family, and service history by subtype.
Disposal proceeds are a credit against the total.

Every figure is bounded by ownership per ruling 12. A lifetime result that would cross
an ownership gap is unavailable with the T5 human reason, and Rover still renders exact
rows for each interval on its own. Event counts obey the same bound as money.

Done-check: a service recorded after a disposal enters neither the total nor the
service count.

### T12 — Corrections

Added after the original ten. Every event could be created and none could be corrected,
so a mistyped total was permanent.

An edit control on every event card, an `edit-event` endpoint, and a correction script.
Corrections update in place; the substrate keeps the history.

Done-check: an event is corrected from its card in a real browser, and the correction
reads back.

**Ruling 22 came out of this task.** The brief told the agent to refuse a correction
that clears the odometer link, citing ruling 11's "a family without an odometer link is
incomplete by definition." The agent checked the create path, found that creation
already accepts a blank mileage, and filed the contradiction rather than building it.
That sentence describes completeness; it does not bind the write path.

## Out of scope

- **Trips and vehicle parts.** The real corpus holds zero of each across three
  years. Fuelly has neither.
- **Attachments in the database.** The corpus holds 109 fill photos and 3 event
  photos. Whether Obelisk should hold blobs is a separate substrate question. The
  converter continues to write a manifest.
- **Community aggregate corpus.** This is sharing purpose 3. It depends on M7
  supplying the service-cost side, so it follows M7 rather than joining it.
- **Leases.** A leased vehicle is neither acquired nor disposed in the ownership
  sense.
- **Consumption entry and the five fixture-only evidence areas.** These stay
  deferred per the 2026-08-11 ruling. They remain inert, and nothing in shipped
  behavior breaks while they wait.
